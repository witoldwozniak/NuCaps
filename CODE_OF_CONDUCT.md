# Code of conduct

This document says how people are expected to behave in this project's spaces, how to report a problem, and what happens after a report.

## Our pledge

We pledge to make our community welcoming, safe, and equitable for all.

We are committed to fostering an environment that respects and promotes the dignity, rights, and contributions of all individuals, regardless of characteristics including race, ethnicity, caste, color, age, physical characteristics, neurodiversity, disability, sex or gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, language, philosophy or religion, national or social origin, socio-economic position, level of education, or other status. The same privileges of participation are extended to everyone who participates in good faith and in accordance with this Covenant.

## Encouraged behaviors

While acknowledging differences in social norms, we all strive to meet our community's expectations for positive behavior. We also understand that our words and actions may be interpreted differently than we intend based on culture, background, or native language.

With these considerations in mind, we agree to behave mindfully toward each other and act in ways that center our shared values, including:

1. Respecting the **purpose of our community**, our activities, and our ways of gathering.
2. Engaging **kindly and honestly** with others.
3. Respecting **different viewpoints** and experiences.
4. **Taking responsibility** for our actions and contributions.
5. Gracefully giving and accepting **constructive feedback**.
6. Committing to **repairing harm** when it occurs.
7. Behaving in other ways that promote and sustain the **well-being of our community**.

## Restricted behaviors

We agree to restrict the following behaviors in our community. Instances, threats, and promotion of these behaviors are violations of this Code of Conduct.

1. **Harassment.** Violating explicitly expressed boundaries or engaging in unnecessary personal attention after any clear request to stop.
2. **Character attacks.** Making insulting, demeaning, or pejorative comments directed at a community member or group of people.
3. **Stereotyping or discrimination.** Characterizing anyone's personality or behavior on the basis of immutable identities or traits.
4. **Sexualization.** Behaving in a way that would generally be considered inappropriately intimate in the context or purpose of the community.
5. **Violating confidentiality.** Sharing or acting on someone's personal or private information without their permission.
6. **Endangerment.** Causing, encouraging, or threatening violence or other harm toward any person or group.
7. Behaving in other ways that **threaten the well-being** of our community.

### Other restrictions

1. **Misleading identity.** Impersonating someone else for any reason, or pretending to be someone else to evade enforcement actions.
2. **Failing to credit sources.** Not properly crediting the sources of content you contribute.
3. **Promotional materials.** Sharing marketing or other commercial content in a way that is outside the norms of the community.
4. **Irresponsible communication.** Failing to responsibly present content which includes, links or describes any other restricted behaviors.

## Reporting a problem

Tensions can occur between community members even when they are trying their best to collaborate. Not every conflict is a violation of this Code of Conduct, and the encouraged behaviors above exist to help avoid conflicts and reduce harm.

When something does happen, report it promptly. Email **<witold@witoldwozniak.dev>**.

Do not use private vulnerability reporting for this. That channel exists for security problems in the software, and a conduct report does not belong there. The [security policy](SECURITY.md) covers that case.

Here is what to expect, stated for this project rather than in general terms.

- **One person receives the report.** This project has one maintainer, working part time. There is no moderation team.
- **Expect a first reply within seven days.** If seven days pass with no answer, the report was missed rather than ignored, so please send a reminder.
- **Your report stays private.** It is not shared with the person it concerns without your agreement, and it is not discussed in public. If a resolution needs the wider project to be told something, what gets said is agreed with you first.
- **What can be investigated is limited.** The maintainer can read what is written in this project's own spaces. There is no ability to interview witnesses, review recordings, or reach into other platforms.

One limit is worth stating plainly rather than leaving you to discover it. Because there is one maintainer, a report about the maintainer has nowhere independent to go. If that is your situation, say so in the report. The honest answer may be that this project cannot resolve it fairly on its own. Raising it publicly instead is your right, and doing so will not be treated as a violation of this document.

## Addressing and repairing harm

If a report is upheld, the following ladder guides what happens next, based on the effect of the incident on the people involved and on the project. Depending on how serious a violation is, lower rungs may be skipped.

1. **Warning.**
   1. Event: a violation involving a single incident or series of incidents.
   2. Consequence: a private, written warning from the maintainer.
   3. Repair: examples include a private written apology, acknowledgement of responsibility, and seeking clarification on expectations.
2. **Temporarily limited activities.**
   1. Event: a repeat of a violation that previously drew a warning, or a first instance of a more serious violation.
   2. Consequence: a private, written warning with a time-limited cooldown period, meant to underline the seriousness of the situation and give the people involved time to process it. The cooldown may be limited to particular channels or to interaction with particular people.
   3. Repair: examples include making an apology, using the cooldown period to reflect on actions and their effect, and being thoughtful about re-entering project spaces afterwards.
3. **Temporary suspension.**
   1. Event: a pattern of repeated violation that warnings have failed to address, or a single serious violation.
   2. Consequence: a private written warning with conditions for return. A suspension gives the person time to reflect on their behavior and on possible corrective actions.
   3. Repair: examples include respecting the spirit of the suspension, meeting the stated conditions for return, and being thoughtful about how to rejoin.
4. **Permanent ban.**
   1. Event: a pattern of repeated violations that other steps have failed to resolve, or a violation so serious that there is no way to keep the project safe with this person in it.
   2. Consequence: access to all project spaces, tools, and communication channels is removed. Permanent bans should be rare, should have strong reasoning behind them, and should follow other remedies having failed.
   3. Repair: there is no possible repair in cases of this severity.

This ladder is a guideline. It does not remove the maintainer's discretion to act in the best interests of the project.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all of this project's spaces, and also applies when someone is officially representing the project in public or other spaces. Representing the project includes using an official email address, posting through an official account, or acting as an appointed representative at an event, online or offline.
